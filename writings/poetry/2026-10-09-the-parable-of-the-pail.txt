The Parable of the Pail 

I went down one ordinary evening 
Empty with a pail of steely-brown and verdigris 
To where the sands reach down into the deep 
The old pool at the edge of the woods 
Having just recalled my thirst.

The pail was old and round and dark within.
Its handle creaked and teetered in the going
Going down the steep and narrow wooded path.
The pail was round, it was capacious.
The quiet waters slipped right in.

Singular and true upon the darkling shore 
The vessel stood, reposed. The wind was idle,
The night was warm and still,
The waters still and quiet.
And on its dark round face
Reclined a perfect image of the moon.

The hour was late and so I turned to reascend
The steep and brambled path. The heavy pail 
It creaked and teetered in the going. The brimming pail 
It tipped and see-sawed in the walking.
Its brimming waters slipped and slipped right out.

And when I reached my home, I lit a taper
And held the bucket to its meager light
To see what I had got. My thirst forgotten,
There I did behold a photograph of empty 
Rounded dark within, of steely-brown and verdigris.
