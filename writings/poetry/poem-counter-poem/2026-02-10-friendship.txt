Friendship 
<subtitle>An ekphrasis on the original by Agnes Martin<\subtitle>

In our once exuberance you pointed to this thing and said
Our friendship is like this.
Because the light in all our rooms was so
Such I deemed the sun
And thus the sun like us.
But see, the subtler hues of that encroaching body
Belie your happy metaphor,
Which plucked too soon from the corbeil the brightest yellow daffodil
And deemed it was the sense of the assemblage.
Wipe the salt from off your lips
And try the thing again.

Recall the crownèd beast, the stuttering animal
Was brought to bleed at casual expenditure
Of thirty tenders, gaudy just like this.
Was our transaction here so different
Which cashed in trifles long before our interest accrued
To buy us back our weal?

And yet, it was with silver the solutioner was sold
And not this ruddy brown which seems now papered,
Dried and stuff, the brown of our autumnal dusk
Which in the candid heat of our belittled end
Was a small casualty for lusty loafing crunching feet.
What of our distrction, our perplexity at him?
The golden figure of this image, the specter 
Tall and hooded, half-concealed and 
Grimacing there behind the purple drape?

Whatever this thing is, whatever it is you will,
Against it I have kept our secret close through nights,
Stowed behind long mantlefolds hung long and low
And deeply delved within a trunk, folded
In the ponderous folds of dapple-grey and dusty quilts,
Lingering longly, darkly in the room
Where once the gleaming gloom of your bronze skin 
With my lips slowly moving, quivering and babbling, did collide.
